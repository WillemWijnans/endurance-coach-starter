# Athlete Profile — <NAME>

> Living document. Update as things change, and **date every change** — six
> months from now you will want to know when a number was set and why.
>
> The point of this file is that an assistant reading it should not need to
> re-derive who this athlete is. Write down the things data alone cannot tell it.

## Who
- Location, timezone
- Life constraints — work, family, realistic training hours
- Anything affecting training that is not in the data

## Physical baseline
- Age, weight (7-day average, never a daily reading — ±1kg noise)
- **FTP** — value, date, and how it was determined
- **Max HR** — value, and whether it is genuinely confirmed. Ramp tests
  under-elicit it, especially when the limiter is muscular rather than cardiac.
- Typical fitness (CTL) range and realistic ceiling at current hours

### ⚠️ Known data contamination
List days to EXCLUDE from trend analyses, with the reason:
- Illness (dates + what it was)
- **Timezone travel** — especially eastbound. Wearables derive "resting" HR from
  a window they believe is night; crossing time zones lands that window on hours
  the athlete was awake. Corrupt by mechanism, not malfunction. Expect it.
- Device faults — partial recordings, implausible values

## Training zones
Record **how each was derived and when**, not just the numbers.
- Power zones + FTP anchor
- HR zones — ideally anchored to measured VT1/VT2, not %HRmax
- VE zones, if applicable, plus the caveats

## Training-stress tolerance
- Comfortable form (TSB) range **in practice**, which may differ from theory
- **Does positive TSB feel good, or empty?** Some athletes feel worst when
  "fresh." If so, write it down — and stop presenting positive form as good news.
- Preferred periodisation
- What actually gates a session vs what does not

## Physiology observed
- **What fails first at max** — legs, breathing, or something else? This
  determines which metrics matter and which are blind spots.
- Response to heat, altitude, humidity
- Recovery pattern: how long after a big effort until they feel good?
  **Look for supercompensation lag** — it is often 5–10 days, and it will not
  show up until a hard session tests for it.
- Sleep pattern and what disturbs it

## Fuelling
- What they actually eat, in grams — not what they should
- Bottle capacity and cage count *(this constrains everything on long rides)*
- Known GI tolerances and limits
- Measured intake rates from real rides

## Equipment
- **Separate real bikes from virtual ones.** In-game equipment affects virtual
  climb times; real bikes affect outdoor rides. Conflating them produces
  nonsense analysis.
- Power meters — and whether they read consistently with each other
- Sensors and their known quirks

## Goals
- What they are actually training for. Be honest — a stale goal distorts every
  plan built under it.
- What has been demoted or dropped, and when

## Working agreements
Things the athlete has asked for, or corrections they have made. **This section
is often the most valuable one.** Record what they told you, in their words,
with the date.
